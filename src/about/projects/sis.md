## The Challenge

Many scientists are deeply concerned about attacks on science, higher education, and democratic institutions.

Yet the culture of science often prepares people to solve problems as individuals rather than members of organized communities. Independence, specialization, and political neutrality can make it difficult to build the relationships, shared strategy, and leadership needed for sustained collective action.

The challenge wasn't simply to mobilize scientists around a single issue.

It was to build an organization where people could move from isolation and concern toward connection, purpose, and long-term leadership.

---

## How We Got There

As Founder and Lead Organizer, I designed Science in Solidarity as both a political home and a leadership development organization.

Rather than treating organizing and leadership development as separate activities, we embedded learning into every part of the organization's design—from onboarding conversations and Action Hours to campaign planning and volunteer leadership.

Every part of the organization was designed around one question:

**How does this help more people grow into leadership?**

---

### Build relationships before asking for action

Every member's journey began with conversation.

Instead of immediately asking people to volunteer, I facilitated one-on-one conversations that explored their motivations, experiences, and hopes for the community.

Those conversations helped people find meaningful ways to contribute while laying the foundation for long-term relationships and shared commitment.

---

### Design leadership into everyday participation

Leadership wasn't something people earned after years of involvement.

It was something they practiced from the beginning.

Action Hours combined political education, relationship-building, and coordinated action while creating opportunities for participants to facilitate discussions, support one another, and gradually take on greater responsibility.

By embedding leadership into everyday organizing, participants developed confidence through practice rather than waiting until they felt "ready."

---

### Build systems that develop people

Rather than concentrating responsibility within a small leadership team, Science in Solidarity was intentionally designed to distribute leadership across volunteers.

We created leadership pathways, collaborative team structures, strategic planning processes, and coalition relationships that helped members develop new skills while strengthening the organization itself.

Every organizational system was intentionally designed to:

- **Strengthen relationships** across the community.
- **Advance shared goals** through coordinated campaigns.
- **Develop new leaders** through meaningful participation.

---

## Outcomes

Science in Solidarity became more than an activist organization.

It became a place where scientists could build relationships, develop organizing skills, and practice collective leadership while responding to urgent threats facing science and democracy.

The organization developed recurring Action Hours, leadership pathways, volunteer teams, strategic planning processes, coalition partnerships, and ongoing political education that supported both immediate campaigns and long-term community building.

Perhaps most importantly, it demonstrated that scientists are eager to lead—not simply as experts, but as organizers, facilitators, educators, and members of democratic communities.
