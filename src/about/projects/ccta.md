## The Challenge

Climate change conversations often happen in silos.

Scientists present research. Artists create emotional experiences. Community organizations mobilize action. Yet these groups rarely have opportunities to learn from one another in the same space.

For the 10th anniversary of Climate Change Theatre Action, we set out to create something different: a community festival where science, theatre, and dialogue could come together to help people imagine possible climate futures—and their own role in creating them.

---

## My Approach

As Festival Producer, I designed and coordinated a two-day community festival that brought together researchers, artists, environmental organizations, and community members across multiple venues.

Rather than treating theatre as entertainment or science as a lecture, we designed the festival as a participatory experience. Every element invited people to move beyond passive observation and into conversation, reflection, and relationship-building.

Participants watched performances, engaged with climate researchers, explored local ecological sites, connected with environmental organizations, and reflected together on what climate action could look like in their own communities.

### Building partnerships

The festival was made possible through collaboration across universities, artists, community organizations, and environmental groups.

Coordinating these partnerships required designing an experience that honored each group's expertise while creating opportunities for genuine exchange rather than parallel programming.

### Designing participatory experiences

Throughout the festival, theatre became a catalyst for conversation rather than an endpoint.

Instead of asking audiences simply to absorb information, we created opportunities for dialogue, collective reflection, and meaning-making after performances and scientific presentations.

These conversations helped participants connect climate science with their own experiences, values, and communities.

<!-- Photo of audience discussion -->

---

### Connecting people with place

The festival extended beyond traditional performance spaces through guided experiences at the Beal Botanical Garden and Corey Marsh Ecological Research Center.

These activities grounded conversations about climate change in local ecosystems, helping participants connect global challenges with the places they call home.

---

## Outcomes

The festival demonstrated how participatory arts can transform science communication from information sharing into relationship building.

By combining theatre, scientific expertise, and facilitated dialogue, participants weren't simply asked to learn about climate change—they were invited to imagine possible futures together, build new relationships, and consider their own role within collective climate action.

The project also strengthened partnerships among scientists, artists, educators, and community organizations, laying groundwork for future collaborations.
